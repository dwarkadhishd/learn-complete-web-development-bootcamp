While paragraph styles can change depending on the medium, an effective academic or explanatory paragraph usually follows a structured four-part format:Topic Sentence: This is the opening statement that introduces the main point or "controlling idea" of the paragraph. It tells the reader exactly what the text will be about.Supporting Details: These sentences build upon the topic sentence. They provide evidence, analysis, data, or descriptive details to support the central argument.Transitions: Words or phrases (such as however, furthermore, or in contrast) that connect sentences logically to maintain a smooth reading flow.Concluding Sentence: This final sentence summarizes the core idea, ties the points together, or transitions smoothly to the next paragraph without introducing new information.

A paragraph is a self-contained unit of writing that consists of one or more sentences unified by a single central topic, idea, or theme. It serves as a foundational building block for essays, articles, and stories by logically breaking text into readable chunks. Visually, a paragraph is distinct because it always begins on a new line, and its start is typically signaled by an indentation or a blank line space.
